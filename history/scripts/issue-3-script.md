# ISSUE 3 — "THE BRIDGE THAT LOOKS FIXED"
### Repair Theory
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–2.5

**Case type:** Returns to the two-witness E/R rhythm after 2.5 deliberately
broke it — and for the first time, E and R aren't just witnesses, they're
the actual subject. This issue answers the question Issue 2.5 closed on:
once you know loss is structural, what does *repair* even mean?

**The loss:** A historic bridge has been rebuilt three times in twenty years,
each time to rave reviews and a ribbon-cutting. Nobody has crossed it in a
decade. It looks exactly like the old photographs. It just doesn't go
anywhere anyone can actually use.

**The taxonomy this issue establishes:**
- **COSMETIC REPAIR** — restores appearance only; doesn't reopen anything.
- **LITERAL REPAIR** — restores the exact prior state faithfully, but the
  prior state doesn't fit present constraints, so it still doesn't reopen
  access.
- **FUNCTIONAL REPAIR** — restores what the bridge *made possible*, even
  where that requires looking different from the original.

**Direct callback:** this issue is the first place the series states the
coherence gap explicitly, by name: **G = E − R.** Not a new concept — the
gap between what's reachable and what's recognized, first staged as E and R
literally boxing in Issue 0, now shown to be exactly what makes a repair
either real or cosmetic.

**The four-form motif for this issue:** the bridge itself —
1. Postcard-perfect at the ribbon-cutting, crowd applauding (Page 1)
2. A dead branch in the reused Fan of Futures — technically present, nothing
   downstream of it (Page 3)
3. An evidence card, compared across all three repair attempts (Page 4)
4. Walked in person, the real obstruction found hidden behind decorative
   railing (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A newspaper clipping look — a crowd at a ribbon-cutting, the
bridge gleaming, a mayor cutting a ribbon. Behind and below the celebration,
in shadow: the bridge deck itself, weeds growing up through the seams at
the far end, clearly untouched by foot traffic in years.

**Panel A:** The mayor, beaming — *"Third restoration in twenty years. Exact
replica, right down to the ironwork."*
**Panel B:** The Detective, looking past him at the weeds — *"Replica of
what, though. The bridge, or the photograph of the bridge?"*
**Panel C:** Close on the weeds growing through the deck seams — small,
easy to miss detail doing the real work of the page.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #3: A BRIDGE THAT WON'T STOP BEING REPAIRED

---

## PAGE 2 — TWO WITNESSES, BACK IN THE RING

*(E and R return as the direct subject rather than background witnesses —
the boxing-ring staging from Issue 0 is earned again here.)*

**Left panel:** E, at the bridge itself now instead of the diner, tape
measure and load-rating chart in hand. Caption: EXECUTABLE COST — *"I don't
care how it looks. Can something actually cross it? Answer's no. Load
rating's a tenth of what it needs to be."*

**Right panel:** R, standing by a display case of the original blueprints
and photographs. Caption: REPAIR COST — *"By every visual and historical
record, this is a faithful repair. Anyone who remembers the old bridge would
recognize this instantly."*

**Center panel, large:** the two of them, face to face, the gap between them
labeled for the first time in the series:
> **G = E − R**
> The Coherence Gap. When it's small, a repair is real. When it's large, a
> repair is a photograph you can stand on.

**Bottom banner:** RECOGNIZABLE AND REACHABLE ARE NOT THE SAME PROMISE.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE FAN, REVISITED

**Full page.** The same continuation-cone device from Issue 1's Page 3,
reused deliberately — but here, most of the cone is dark not because
futures were merely uncounted, but because a single dead branch near the
mouth of the cone blocks everything downstream of it. *Second appearance of
the bridge — as that one dead branch, technically present, leading nowhere.*

**Sidebar:**
- WHAT THE REPAIR RESTORED — the branch itself, visually intact.
- WHAT THE REPAIR DIDN'T REACH — everything that used to be downstream of
  crossing the bridge: the market on the far bank, the shortcut to the
  school, the trail beyond.

**Bottom caption:**
> Fixing the branch doesn't automatically reopen what was downstream of it.
> Repair that stops at the branch is repair that stops too soon.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** three evidence cards, one per repair attempt:

**REPAIR #1 — COSMETIC** — a photo of freshly painted railings over a
visibly sagging deck.
Card caption: *Looked new. Was never tested.*

**REPAIR #2 — LITERAL** — blueprints laid exactly over the original 1920s
design, unchanged, load ratings from the original era still stamped on the
plans.
Card caption: *Faithful to a bridge built for horse carts, not trucks.*
*Third appearance of the bridge, here as a direct comparison across all
three attempts.*

**REPAIR #3 — the current one, still COSMETIC/LITERAL hybrid** — new paint
over the same old bones, ironwork replicated down to the rivets, load
rating never actually re-engineered.
Card caption: *Prettier than #2. Just as unusable.*

**Bottom banner:** THREE REPAIRS. THE SAME BRIDGE NEVER GOT ANY MORE
CROSSABLE.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective walking the bridge deck himself,
pulling back a decorative railing panel to reveal a rusted, load-bearing
truss hidden underneath — original, corroded, never actually replaced
despite three "restorations." *Fourth appearance of the bridge — the real
obstruction found, hidden behind the parts that got all the attention.*

**Small insets:**
Records say: ✔ Fully restored, three times.
Structure says: ❌ Never actually re-engineered underneath.

**Final panel:** The Detective standing at the exact midpoint of the bridge,
looking toward the far bank, market and school still visible but unreachable.
> Caption: They kept repairing what people could see. Nobody repaired what
> people needed.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The preservation society, insisting — *"Any change to the
original design and it isn't really the bridge anymore."*
**Panel B:** The engineer, frustrated — *"Any repair that keeps the original
design and it isn't really a bridge anymore either. Not one you can use."*
**Panel C:** The Detective between them.

**Visual centerpiece:** The boxing ring from Issue 0, staged one more time
— E and R in the ring again, but this time not fighting. Standing side by
side, looking out at the crowd, both gesturing at the same gap.

**Detective:**
> "You can restore what people recognize, or you can restore what people
> can do. Best case, those are the same repair. This bridge proves they
> don't have to be. Pick one on purpose, or you'll keep doing this every
> twenty years."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective, the boxing ring behind him now
empty, E and R's gloves hung up rather than raised.

**Three giant icons, row:**
- COSMETIC — a fresh coat of paint over a crack.
- LITERAL — an exact blueprint stamped "UNCHANGED."
- FUNCTIONAL — a redesigned truss, different from the original, holding
  weight the original never could.

**Giant text:** REPAIR IS NOT RESTORING A STATE. IT IS RESTORING FUTURES.

**Small line, callback to Page 2:**
> G = E − R. Close the gap, and it doesn't matter which side you started
> from.

**Bottom strip:** same five domain icons, now each shown mid-repair — a
scar healing wrong versus healing functional, a patched codebase that
compiles but doesn't run, etc.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** A fourth construction crew, this time working on the actual
truss underneath, ironwork removed and set carefully aside rather than
discarded.

**Middle panel:** The finished bridge — visibly different from the original
photograph in the load-bearing sections, but the historic ironwork restored
on the railings where it was never structural to begin with. Both societies
present at this ribbon-cutting, both satisfied for different reasons.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, and for the first time in the series,
foot traffic — actual people crossing the bridge in the foreground, headed
toward the market and the school on the far bank.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing about this bridge needed to look like the old one everywhere. It
> needed to go somewhere again. Now it does.

**Footer:** IT WAS NEVER BROKEN. IT WAS JUST REPAIRED AT THE WRONG LAYER.

## Production notes

- This is the first issue to explicitly name G = E − R on the page. Earlier
  issues implied the gap without labeling it; this is where the series
  cashes that check. Keep the notation visually consistent with how it
  might appear in the source paper if you ever want a version of this page
  to double as a plain explainer for non-comic contexts.
- The boxing ring returning on Page 6 — this time with E and R *not*
  fighting — is meant to complete something Issue 0 opened. Worth deciding
  now whether every future issue that revisits the ring should show them
  in a different relationship (allies here, maybe indifferent or divided
  differently elsewhere), so the ring itself becomes a barometer readers
  can track across the series rather than a static logo.
- Resist making the preservation society villainous. Like Issue 2.5, both
  sides here are right about something real — the society is correct that
  the ironwork mattered, the engineer is correct that the truss had to
  change. The failure was treating those as the same demand for twenty
  years, not either side lying.
