# ISSUE 2.5 — "THE HOUSE ON NO MAP"
### CLIO — what is lost when a world is projected into a representation
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–2

**Case type:** A structural variant on the format. Issues 1 and 2 both
resolved by finding someone who quietly let something lapse — a city office,
a numismatic society. This issue's twist: there's no negligence to find.
Nobody dropped the ball. The loss is what projection *costs*, unconditionally,
every time. This is the first issue where the Detective is wrong about there
being a culprit, and has to notice that on the page.

**The loss:** A family insists their house has stood at a certain address
for three generations. The road map shows no such address. The tax rolls
show it clearly. An aerial survey shows a building, unlabeled. Three
authorities, three different answers, and none of them are lying.

**No three-part taxonomy of failure this issue** — deliberately breaking the
pattern the reader now expects from Issues 1 and 2. Instead, one structural
claim: **every projection keeps something and drops something, and the drop
is not a mistake.** (Issue 3 picks up the active response to this: if loss is
structural, what does *repair* even mean?)

**The four-form motif for this issue:** the house itself, shown four times
through four different projections —
1. Named, address and all, in the family's own account (Page 1)
2. Present on the tax map, absent from the road map — literally blank space
   where it should be (Page 3, the fan reused as overlapping projections)
3. A dot on the aerial photo, unlabeled, indistinguishable from any other
   roof (Page 4, evidence card)
4. Located in person, all three projections finally laid over one another
   and lined up (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A family standing on their porch, house behind them, ordinary
in every way. In the foreground, a road map spread on a folding table, the
street and house entirely absent from it — the paper just continues past
where the house should be, blank.

**Panel A:** The eldest family member — *"We've lived here sixty years. The
county says this address doesn't exist."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, looking between the house and the map —
*"The county isn't lying. That's what worries me."*
**Panel C:** A close, disorienting shot: the map's blank paper exactly where
the porch should be, as though the house had been cut out with scissors.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #2.5: A HOUSE THE MAP FORGOT TO ARGUE ABOUT

---

## PAGE 2 — THREE WITNESSES, NOT TWO

*(Deliberate break from the E/R two-witness pattern — this issue needs a
third voice, because the mystery is about more than two kinds of cost.)*

**Panel A:** The Cartographer, road map spread out — *"I draw what you can
drive to. This address isn't on a road my map recognizes as public."*
**Panel B:** The Assessor, ledger open, tax rolls — *"I draw what's owned.
Far as I'm concerned, that house has paid property tax since before I was
born."*
**Panel C:** The Surveyor, aerial photographs pinned up — *"I draw what's
visible from above. There's a roof there. I couldn't tell you a name for it
if you paid me."*

**Center caption, small:**
> Three honest maps. Three different houses missing.

**Bottom banner:** NOBODY HERE IS WRONG. THAT'S THE PROBLEM.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE OVERLAY

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** The three maps — road, tax, aerial —
rendered as translucent overlapping sheets, each shot through with light
like x-ray film, laid one on top of the other. Where all three agree,
solid ink. Where they diverge, each sheet shows something the others don't:
a road with no tax record, a tax lot with no road, a rooftop with neither.

*Second appearance of the house — a blank gap precisely where two of the
three sheets fail to overlap.*

**Sidebar:**
- KEPT BY ALL THREE — the load-bearing facts everyone agrees on.
- KEPT BY ONE — real, but visible only from a single angle.
- KEPT BY NONE — real in the world, invisible in every available
  projection.

**Bottom caption:**
> Every map is a kind of honesty. No map can be honest about everything at
> once.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** corkboard, but this time only two cards instead of three —
visually signaling to the returning reader that the usual taxonomy doesn't
apply here.

**WHAT GOT KEPT** — three swatches side by side: the road, the tax lot, the
roof. *Third appearance of the house, as an unlabeled dot on the aerial
swatch.*
Card caption: *Every projection saved something true.*

**WHAT GOT DROPPED** — the same three swatches, this time showing what's
missing from each: no address on the road map, no shape on the tax map, no
name on the aerial photo.
Card caption: *Every projection discarded something true, too.*

**Center, large text between the two cards:** THIS ISN'T A COVER-UP.

**Bottom banner:** NO SINGLE PICTURE WAS EVER GOING TO BE ENOUGH.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective standing at the actual address, all
three maps held up together, physically aligning their edges against the
real skyline. *Fourth appearance of the house — located, for the first
time, by triangulating all three sources at once rather than trusting any
one of them.*

**Small insets:**
Road map: ❌ no address.
Tax roll: ✔ owned, taxed, on record.
Aerial: ✔ visible, unlabeled.

**Final panel:** The Detective marking a new symbol on a blank fourth sheet
— not erasing the discrepancy, just finally writing all three answers down
in the same place.
> Caption: The house was never missing. It was only ever in one projection
> at a time.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE NON-CONFRONTATION

*(No adversary this issue — the "confrontation" page becomes a reflection
instead, another deliberate break from the pattern.)*

**Panel A:** Cartographer, Assessor, and Surveyor, all three now standing
together at the site, looking a little sheepish but not guilty.
**Panel B:** The Detective, notebook out, addressing all three at once.

**Visual centerpiece:** No scale this issue. Instead: three transparent
sheets, held up to a single light source, the beam passing through all
three and landing, undivided, on the house.

**Detective:**
> "None of you left anything out on purpose. You each kept what your job
> needed. Nobody's job was 'keep everything.' That job doesn't exist. That's
> not a flaw in any one of your maps. It's what a map *is.*"

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective facing the reader, the three
overlapping translucent sheets from Page 3 behind him instead of the usual
single cone or tree.

**No three-icon row this issue.** Instead, one large central image: a
single object (a simple house shape) refracted through a prism into three
differently-colored projections, none of them the full white light.

**Giant text:** EVERY PROJECTION KEEPS SOMETHING AND LOSES SOMETHING. THE
LOSS ISN'T A MISTAKE. IT'S THE PRICE OF A PICTURE.

**Small line beneath, setting up Issue 3:**
> The next question isn't who's to blame for what a map drops.
> It's what you do once you know it dropped something. That's a different
> kind of case.

**Bottom strip:** same five domain icons as Issues 1–2 (city, codebase,
brain, institution, language) — now each shown with three small overlapping
translucent swatches instead of one solid image, tying the visual language
back to the running motif.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** The family's address, now recorded in a new combined ledger —
road access, tax status, and aerial location, all cross-referenced instead
of consolidated into one "true" version.

**Middle panel:** The three original maps, still separate, still each
incomplete — but pinned together now, edges aligned, rather than treated as
competitors.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, its beam this time shown passing
through the three translucent map-sheets before reaching the water —
refracted, not blocked.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing was hidden here. Everything was just told honestly, in pieces,
> by people who each only had one piece to tell.

**Footer:** THE HOUSE WAS NEVER LOST. IT WAS ONLY EVER PARTIAL.

## Production notes

- This issue should *feel* different on a skim, not just read differently —
  three witnesses instead of two, two evidence cards instead of three, no
  scale on Page 6, no icon-triad on Page 7. A returning reader who's picked
  up the rhythm of Issues 1–2 should notice the absence of the usual devices
  before they can articulate why. That felt absence is the content: this
  case genuinely doesn't have the shape the last two did.
- Resist the urge to give this issue a villain. The temptation will be to
  make the Cartographer or Assessor cagey/defensive the way Issue 1 and 2's
  officials were. Page 6 explicitly needs all three to be sympathetic and
  correct — if any of them reads as hiding something, the issue's central
  point (loss without fault) collapses back into the previous issues'
  format instead of standing apart from it.
- Page 7's closing line is the explicit hinge into Issue 3 — it should be
  the only place in the whole issue that gestures forward rather than just
  closing the case. Keep it short; one sentence, not a preview panel.
