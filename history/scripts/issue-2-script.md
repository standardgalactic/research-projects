# ISSUE 2 — "TWO THINGS, ONE NAME"
### Ecology of Distinctions
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issue 1 v2

**Case type:** A distinction has gone missing — parallel structure to Issue 1,
but the object lost isn't a future, it's the ability to tell two things
apart.

**The loss:** A numismatic society declares two rare banknotes "effective
twins — for all practical purposes, the same note." The Detective has to
determine whether the difference between them was genuinely erased, or
whether it just stopped being maintained.

**The three-part taxonomy this issue establishes** (parallel to Issue 1's
Constraint / Projection / Reachability, but for distinctions rather than
futures):
- **MERGE** — the difference is genuinely gone; nothing left distinguishes
  them even in principle.
- **DRIFT** — a test still exists, but it no longer tracks what it used to;
  the boundary moved without anyone updating the label.
- **NEGLECT** — the difference is still detectable, but maintaining the
  ability to detect it costs upkeep nobody's paid in years.

**The four-form motif for this issue:** one faint identifying mark on the
banknotes, shown four times —
1. Absent from the appraiser's side-by-side comparison (Page 1)
2. A faded, barely-forking branch in the distinction-tree (Page 3)
3. An evidence card under DRIFT (Page 4)
4. Rediscovered under a loupe / UV light, faint but present (Page 5)

Do not caption the recurrence anywhere in-panel — same rule as Issue 1.

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A velvet display case, two banknotes laid side by side under
glass, identical at a glance. An appraiser's loupe rests beside them, unused.

**Panel A:** The appraiser, adjusting his glasses — *"Might as well be the
same note. Nobody's told them apart in thirty years."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, unconvinced — *"'Nobody has' isn't the
same claim as 'nobody can.'"*
**Panel C:** Tight crop on the two notes, a barely-visible hairline
difference in the corner ornament — easy to miss, meant to be missed on
first read.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #2: A DISTINCTION HAS GONE MISSING

---

## PAGE 2 — TWO WITNESSES

**Top-left panel:** E, at the diner booth again, banknotes spread instead of
street maps. Caption: EXECUTABLE COST — *"I can tell you what it'd cost to
run every known test on these. That cost isn't infinite. It's just gone
unpaid."*

**Top-right panel:** R, at his desk, an old cataloguer's ledger open in front
of him, a newer one beside it with a blank column where a distinction used to
be logged. Caption: REPAIR COST — *"Somewhere between this ledger and that
one, somebody stopped writing the difference down. That's not the same as
the difference not existing."*

**Center inset:** The Detective's notebook —
> Untested ≠ untestable.
> Unrecorded ≠ nonexistent.

**Bottom panel:** E and R together at the booth, both looking at the same
faint hairline mark now blown up between them on a photograph.

**Bottom banner:** TWO WITNESSES. NEITHER ONE SAYS "GONE."

---

## PAGE 3 — THE DISTINCTION TREE

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** A garden path splitting into smaller and
smaller forks — a tree of distinctions rather than a cone of futures. Most
forks are clean and well-worn. Some are overgrown, barely visible under
brush. One fork, near the bottom, has genuinely closed — grown over solid,
no path at all.

**Sidebar, three categories:**
- MAINTAINED DISTINCTIONS — worn paths, clearly forked.
- DRIFTING DISTINCTIONS — forks still there, overgrown, hard to find.
- MERGED DISTINCTIONS — no fork left at all; genuinely one path now.

**Small inset diagram** (bottom corner, echoing Issue 1's cone-diagram):
simple branching tree, one branch shown fading from solid to dotted to
absent.

**Bottom caption:**
> A distinction doesn't have to be false to disappear.
> It only has to stop being maintained.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** corkboard, three evidence cards:

**MERGE** — two coins melted into a single ingot, side by side with the
original coin designs barely legible in the metal.
Card caption: *There's nothing left to tell apart.*

**DRIFT** — an old identification test (say, a watermark chart) next to a
modern one, with an arrow showing the modern test now flags a *different*
feature than the original test tracked. *Third appearance of the mark, now
as evidence under this card.*
Card caption: *The test moved. The label didn't.*

**NEGLECT** — a locked cabinet, dust thick on the handle, a maintenance
log with the last entry decades old.
Card caption: *The tool to tell them apart still works. Nobody's opened the
cabinet.*

**Bottom banner:** THEY LOOK IDENTICAL FROM FAR AWAY. ONLY THE UPKEEP IS
DIFFERENT.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective under a desk lamp, loupe to his eye,
UV light in hand, examining the two banknotes directly. *Fourth appearance
of the mark — found, faint but real, under direct examination.*

**Small insets:**
Ledger says: ❌ No distinguishing feature on record.
Note says: ✔ Feature present, faint, never erased.

**Final panel:** The Detective holding both notes up to the light, mark now
clearly visible between them.
> Caption: The distinction lives in the object, not the catalogue entry.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The appraiser, defensive, arms crossed over the display case.
**Panel B:** The Detective laying the Page 4 evidence cards on the counter.
**Panel C:** E and R standing behind him again, expert-witness staging.

**Visual centerpiece:** A scale, off-balance. One side: DISTINGUISHABLE
(weighted, full). Other side: WORTH MAINTAINING (light, nearly empty). Same
visual device as Issue 1's scale, now applied to a different question —
deliberately rhymed so the reader recognizes the shape of the argument
before reading the labels.

**Detective:**
> "You didn't decide these were the same note. You just decided the
> difference wasn't worth the upkeep. Those aren't the same decision, and
> only one of them was actually yours to make quietly."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective facing the reader, the distinction-
tree from Page 3 behind him, the lighthouse on the horizon.

**Three giant icons, row:**
- MERGE — two shapes fused into one.
- DRIFT — a fork with the label pointing the wrong direction.
- NEGLECT — a locked, dusty cabinet.

**Giant text:** DISTINCTIONS DON'T VANISH ON THEIR OWN. THEY GO UNMAINTAINED.

**Bottom strip:** small icons echoing Issue 1's — a city, a codebase, a
brain, an institution, a language — same five domains, signaling this
triad applies everywhere the last issue's triad did, just one level down
(what survives, rather than what remains reachable).

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** The society's ledger, reopened, the missing column restored,
a new entry filled in by hand.

**Middle panel:** The two banknotes, relabeled — not "identical," not
"different," but tagged with the actual distinguishing feature, in plain
language.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse again, the two notes laid on a table
in the foreground, mark now visible in the lighthouse's beam.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing here was ever the same thing twice. Something was just left
> untested long enough to be mistaken for identical.

**Footer:** IT WAS NEVER ONE NOTE. IT WAS TWO, UNMAINTAINED.

## Production notes

- This issue's triad (Merge / Drift / Neglect) is a direct structural sibling
  of Issue 1's (Constraint / Projection / Reachability), and Page 6's scale
  is drawn to visually rhyme with Issue 1's — same device, different labels.
  That repetition is deliberate: by Issue 3 the reader should start
  anticipating "there will be a three-part taxonomy and an off-balance
  scale," which is the moment the series' proof-structure becomes visible
  as a structure rather than a coincidence.
- E and R's role this issue is smaller and more collegial than Issue 1 —
  they agree with each other by Page 2's bottom panel, which is new. Worth
  keeping: their disagreement was the engine of Issue 1's investigation,
  but this issue's tension is between the Detective and the appraiser, not
  between E and R. Don't force conflict between them where the case doesn't
  need it.
- The mark itself should be genuinely hard to see on Page 1 and Page 3 —
  if it's too legible early, the Page 5 "discovery" loses its payoff.
