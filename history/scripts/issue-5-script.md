# ISSUE 5 — "NOBODY WAS AIMING AT THAT BLOCK"
### Preference Fields
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–4.5

**Case type:** Answers the question Issue 4.5 closed on — if a shape keeps
winning across every scale, what's it winning *toward*? This issue's answer:
not a target. A field. The distinction the whole issue turns on:

- **TARGET** — a single point everyone is aiming at, explicitly or in
  collusion.
- **FIELD** — many small, local, independent pulls that add up to a common
  drift, with no destination anyone actually chose.

**The loss — or rather, the accusation:** A city councilmember presents ten
years of resettlement data: residents from dozens of different starting
neighborhoods, all converging disproportionately onto the same six-block
stretch. She's convinced a developer engineered it — steered people there on
purpose. The Detective has to determine whether there's a hidden target, or
whether something more like gravity is doing the work.

**The four-form motif:** the six-block stretch itself, shown four times —
1. As a single arrow-converged point on the councilmember's exhibit map,
   implying deliberate steering (Page 1)
2. As the "magnet" in an iron-filings overlay on the city map — many small
   trajectories bending, none of them pointed straight at it (Page 3)
3. Broken into three separate, unglamorous gradient maps — price, safety,
   amenities — none of which alone explains the convergence (Page 4)
4. Explained, resident by resident, by people who never once mention the
   block as a destination (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A city council chamber. A large exhibit map on an easel: ten
years of resident relocation lines, hundreds of them, thin and colored by
year, all bending inward toward the same six blocks like water down a drain.

**Panel A:** The councilmember, pointing — *"Ten years. Every line ends up
in the same six blocks. Somebody built that on purpose."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, studying the converging lines — *"A
lot of things can look aimed. Rivers look aimed at the sea. Nobody's
steering the river."*
**Panel C:** Close on the exhibit map's legend — dozens of unrelated
starting points, no two alike.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #5: EVERYONE ENDED UP IN THE SAME PLACE. NOBODY
AGREES ON WHY.

---

## PAGE 2 — TARGET OR FIELD

**Panel A:** The developer, hands spread — *"I never marketed to a single
block. I don't even own half the property down there."*
**Panel B:** A city demographer, spreadsheets out — *"The convergence is
real. I've checked it six ways. I just can't find anyone who planned it."*
**Center caption panel:**
> TARGET: one point, chosen, aimed at.
> FIELD: many small pulls, none of them chosen alone, adding up to the same
> drift anyway.
> Both produce the same map. Only one of them has a suspect.

**Bottom banner:** THE SHAPE OF THE EVIDENCE DOESN'T TELL YOU WHICH ONE
YOU'RE LOOKING AT.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE FIELD, MADE VISIBLE

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** The city map transformed: iron filings
scattered across it, each one a tiny resident-trajectory arrow, all subtly
bending toward the six-block stretch the way filings bend around a magnet
— without any single filing pointing directly at it. *Second appearance of
the block, now as the "magnet" position, visually pulling without visibly
touching any one line.*

**Sidebar:**
- WHAT A TARGET LOOKS LIKE — a single bold arrow, straight line, one
  source to one destination.
- WHAT A FIELD LOOKS LIKE — hundreds of short, locally bent arrows, no
  single one aimed at anything in particular, all leaning the same
  direction anyway.

**Bottom caption:**
> Nothing here is aimed. Everything here is leaning.
> A field doesn't need a single believer. It only needs enough small
> agreements.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** three gradient maps pinned side by side instead of a
taxonomy of failure — *third appearance of the block, broken apart into the
forces actually pulling on it:*

**PRICE GRADIENT** — a heat map, rent cooling gradually as you approach the
six blocks.
Card caption: *A little cheaper, the closer you get. Not free. Just less
expensive than staying.*

**SAFETY GRADIENT** — a second heat map, incident reports thinning the same
direction.
Card caption: *A little safer, the closer you get. Nobody chose this
consciously. Everybody noticed it anyway.*

**AMENITY GRADIENT** — a third heat map, park access and transit density
both rising toward the same blocks.
Card caption: *A little more convenient, the closer you get.*

**Center, large text beneath the three cards:** NONE OF THESE ALONE
EXPLAINS THE MAP. ADDED TOGETHER, THEY DO.

**Bottom banner:** THREE WEAK PULLS. ONE STRONG DRIFT.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective on an actual porch in the six-block
stretch, notebook out, interviewing residents one at a time. *Fourth
appearance of the block — heard about secondhand, from people who moved
there for reasons that have nothing to do with the block itself.*

**Three small speech-bubble insets, different residents:**
> "Rent dropped forty dollars a month when I crossed that street."
> "My sister said it felt safer walking home at night."
> "Bus stop's closer. That's genuinely the whole reason."

**Final panel:** The Detective, closing his notebook.
> Caption: Not one of them said the block's name like it was a destination.
> They all said it like it was just where the small reasons ran out of
> resistance.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The councilmember, still unconvinced — *"Someone had to draw
that map first. Coincidence doesn't look this clean."*
**Panel B:** The Detective, laying Page 4's three gradient maps on top of
each other, showing how their overlap alone reproduces the exhibit map from
Page 1 almost exactly.
**Panel C:** The developer and demographer, both watching the overlay
resolve.

**Visual centerpiece:** the iron-filings map from Page 3 again, this time
with the "magnet" removed entirely from the drawing — the filings still
bend the same way, held only by the three combined gradients underneath.

**Detective:**
> "You don't need a magnet for filings to line up. You need enough small
> forces pointed roughly the same way. Nobody built your target. Three
> ordinary things quietly agreed with each other for ten years, and that's
> what you're looking at."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective facing the reader, the iron-filings
city map behind him, no magnet drawn anywhere in the image.

**No icon triad this issue — one large central diagram:** a single point
labeled TARGET crossed out, replaced by a smooth gradient field with many
small arrows all leaning the same direction, none of them long.

**Giant text:** PREFERENCE IS NOT A DESTINATION. IT IS A FIELD THAT BENDS
WHICH FUTURES LOOK REACHABLE AT ALL.

**Small line, forward gesture into Issue 6:**
> A field this persistent has to be held somewhere between one measurement
> and the next. That holding-in-place is its own question. That's next.

**Bottom strip:** the five domain icons again, now each shown with small
bending arrows instead of a single arrow — signaling that "field, not
target" applies to institutions, language, and cognition as much as to a
city block.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** The council chamber again, the exhibit map now annotated
with the three gradient overlays instead of a single accusing arrow.

**Middle panel:** A new city planning document — not "stop the developer,"
but "the price, safety, and amenity gradients are real, plan for them
directly instead of chasing whoever you think is causing them."

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, its beam this time shown bending
subtly through the harbor mist rather than traveling in a straight line —
still reaching the water, just along a curve instead of an arrow.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing here was steered. Everything here leaned, a little, in the same
> direction, for long enough that it started looking like a decision.

**Footer:** NOBODY WAS AIMING AT THAT BLOCK. THE WHOLE CITY WAS LEANING
TOWARD IT.

---

## Production notes

- This issue lives or dies on Page 3 and Page 6 rhyming visually — the
  iron-filings map has to appear twice, once with an implied "magnet" and
  once with the magnet conspicuously absent, so the reader feels the pull
  get explained rather than explained away. Don't let Page 6 skip straight
  to the developer being cleared without showing that second, magnet-less
  version of the map.
- Resist giving the councilmember a "gotcha" moment where she's proven
  foolish. Her instinct — that a pattern this clean needs an author — is
  the correct instinct in Issues 1 and 4.5's kind of case. This issue's
  point is that the same clean pattern can also come from a field, not
  that suspicion of clean patterns is generally wrong.
- Page 5's resident quotes should stay small and mundane on purpose —
  forty dollars, a safer walk, a closer bus stop. If any resident's stated
  reason sounds even slightly like it's describing the block as a
  destination, the page undercuts its own argument.
