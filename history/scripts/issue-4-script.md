# ISSUE 4 — "THE TWO MACHINES"
### Continuation Geometry: the shape of what can still happen
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–3

**Case type:** Where Issue 3 answered "what does repair restore," this issue
asks the question that makes repair worth measuring in the first place: how
do you tell how much future something has left, when the present moment
looks the same either way?

**Note on adaptation:** unlike 1–3, this issue already exists as a
standalone infographic ("Continuation Geometry — The Shape of What Can Still
Happen"). This script converts that material into the series' case-file
format rather than inventing new content — Panels 1, 5, 9, and 14 of the
original poster map directly onto this issue's cold open, measuring-board,
evidence, and closing pages.

**The loss:** An auction house is liquidating a warehouse of dormant
machines, all tagged "identical condition, identical value" based on a
single inspection photo. Two units in particular, side by side, look
indistinguishable — same model, same posture, same layer of dust.

**This issue's device — four instruments instead of a three-part taxonomy:**
- **VOLUME** — how many distinct futures remain reachable at all.
- **DISTANCE** — how far the nearest genuinely different future is.
- **DENSITY** — how tightly packed the reachable futures are, versus how
  sparse.
- **CURVATURE** — whether nearby futures fan outward or funnel back toward
  each other.

**The four-form motif:** the two machines' internal wiring, shown four
times —
1. Identical from the outside, tagged with one shared auction lot number
   (Page 1)
2. As two very differently shaped cones in the reused Fan of Futures — one
   wide open, one nearly collapsed to a line (Page 3)
3. Measured instrument-by-instrument on the evidence board (Page 4)
4. Opened up and inspected directly, the actual internal difference finally
   visible (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A warehouse auction floor. Two machines, side by side, same
make, same dust, same idle posture — "sleeping," to the eye. A single
shared tag hangs between them: LOT 14 — IDENTICAL CONDITION.

**Panel A:** The auctioneer, gavel raised — *"Matching pair. Whatever one's
worth, so's the other."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, studying them both without touching
either — *"A photograph can't tell rest from ruin. That's not a flaw in the
photograph. It's a flaw in stopping there."*
**Panel C:** Extreme close-up — one machine has a faint cobweb across an
access panel the other doesn't.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #4: TWO MACHINES, ONE PRICE TAG

---

## PAGE 2 — WHAT A SNAPSHOT CAN'T SAY

**Left panel:** Machine A, labeled RESTING, small "Z Z Z" over its dormant
frame.
**Right panel:** Machine B, labeled EXHAUSTED, cobwebs, a single dead
warning light.
**Center caption panel, large text:**
> A snapshot cannot tell the difference between rest and ruin.
> What matters is not what a system is doing right now.
> What matters is how many futures remain open to it.

**Bottom banner:** STATE ≠ POSSIBILITY.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE FAN, TWICE

**Full page,** the continuation cone from Issue 1 reused, but split into two
side-by-side versions springing from each machine:

Machine A's cone: wide, branching, dozens of live paths fanning outward,
brightly lit.
Machine B's cone: nearly collapsed — a thin, dark sliver, most branches
already dead ends. *Second appearance of the wiring — visible faintly
inside each cone's mouth, healthy and tangled in A's, scorched and singular
in B's.*

**Bottom caption:**
> Same lot number. Same price tag. Not remotely the same amount of future
> left.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE MEASURING BOARD

*(Replaces the usual three-card investigation board with four instrument
readings — this issue's structural signature.)*

**Full page,** a surveyor's table, four instruments laid out, each with a
reading for both machines side by side:

**VOLUME** — a dial. A: wide range. B: barely off zero.
Caption: *How many futures are reachable at all.*

**DISTANCE** — a measuring tape stretched to two very different lengths.
Caption: *How far to the nearest genuinely different outcome.*

**DENSITY** — a grid overlay, A's crowded with dots, B's nearly empty.
Caption: *How tightly packed the possibilities are.*

**CURVATURE** — a simple curved-line diagram, A's fanning outward, B's
folding back on itself. *Third appearance of the wiring, laid faintly under
each instrument reading as the thing actually being measured.*
Caption: *Whether nearby futures spread apart or collapse into each other.*

**Bottom banner:** FOUR INSTRUMENTS. NONE OF THEM ASK WHAT THE MACHINE
LOOKS LIKE RIGHT NOW.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective, panel removed from each machine,
looking directly at the wiring inside. *Fourth appearance of the wiring —
Machine A's intact and reconnectable; Machine B's fused solid at a single
critical junction, foreclosing nearly everything downstream.*

**Small insets:**
Auction tag says: ✔ Identical condition.
Wiring says: A — reachable. B — one junction from total collapse.

**Final panel:** The Detective closing both panels back up, expression
grim.
> Caption: They weren't lying about the dust. They just never asked what
> was still live underneath it.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The auctioneer, defensive — *"They look the same. They ran the
same. What else is there?"*
**Panel B:** The Detective, laying the four instrument readings on the
table.
**Panel C:** A buyer, mid-bid, pausing with hand half-raised.

**Visual centerpiece:** A scale again — but this time weighing two objects
that look identical on the surface (two matching machine silhouettes), with
a second, hidden pair of scales beneath showing wildly different weights
once the wiring is factored in.

**Detective:**
> "You can sell what a thing is right now. Or you can sell what it can
> still become. Selling one lot number for both means somebody's about to
> pay for a future that machine doesn't have left."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective, the two split cones from Page 3
behind him, one wide and lit, one collapsed and dark.

**Four instrument icons, row (replacing the usual three):**
VOLUME — DISTANCE — DENSITY — CURVATURE.

**Giant text:** FUTURES ARE NOT JUST SETS. THEY HAVE GEOMETRY.

**Small line, callback and forward gesture:**
> Repair (Issue 3) told us what's worth restoring.
> Continuation geometry tells us how to tell, before you even try, whether
> there's anything left to restore.

**Bottom strip:** same five domain icons (city, codebase, brain,
institution, language), now each shown with a small pair of split cones —
one wide, one collapsed — signaling the same measurement question applies
everywhere.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** The auction floor, the shared lot number struck through,
two new tags issued — separately priced, separately described.

**Middle panel:** Machine A, sold to a buyer with a full service plan.
Machine B, sold separately, honestly, as salvage.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, the two machines now shown at a
distance from each other — one being wheeled toward a workshop full of
possible futures, one toward a scrapyard, both endings honest ones.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing was mislabeled out of malice. It's just that a price tag can only
> describe a moment, and these two machines were never standing in the same
> future.

**Footer:** THEY LOOKED THE SAME. THEY WERE NEVER THE SAME SHAPE.

## Production notes

- This issue trades the three-icon taxonomy for four measuring instruments,
  deliberately — it's the first issue about *measurement* rather than
  *classification*, and the format should feel like a survey instead of an
  evidence board. Keep the surveyor's-table staging on Page 4 distinct from
  the corkboard staging used in Issues 1–3.
- The split-cone image on Page 3 is the load-bearing visual for the whole
  issue — if a reader only remembers one image from Issue 4, it should be
  the two cones, one wide and lit, one collapsed and dark, sharing a single
  price tag. Consider using a version of it as this issue's cover, the way
  the barricade served Issue 1.
- Machine framing (rather than patients, twins, or other living subjects)
  is deliberate and worth keeping — it lets the "snapshot vs. possibility"
  point land cleanly without the reader importing real medical or personal
  stakes that this issue isn't equipped to handle responsibly. If a future
  issue wants to make this same point about a person, it needs its own
  script, not a reskin of this one.
