# ISSUE 10 — "THE NOVEL THE ALGORITHM WOULDN'T FINISH"
### Terminal and Traversal — what reading actually costs
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–9

**Note on adaptation:** like Issue 4, this issue already exists as a
standalone infographic ("Terminal and Traversal — A Theory of What Reading
Actually Costs"). This script converts that material into the series'
case-file format rather than inventing new content — the Recognition-vs-
Traversal reader panel and the tortoise-and-hare selection-pressure panel
both map directly onto this issue's witness page and evidence board.

**Case type:** Another "no villain" issue, sibling to 2.5 and 4.5 — the
accusation looks like deliberate suppression and resolves into structural
selection pressure instead.

**The loss — or rather, the accusation:** A serialized novelist accuses a
reading platform of burying her work — chapter views collapsing every
release, while another writer's self-contained daily anecdotes get boosted
relentlessly. She wants the algorithm audited for censorship. The Detective
has to determine whether anyone chose to suppress her, or whether something
about *what her writing asks of a reader* is losing a race nobody's
running on purpose.

**Direct terms from the source poster, kept intact:**
- **Recognition** — classify a segment, match it to a model, move on.
  Settled at first contact. Most reading is this.
- **Traversal** — hold a segment's meaning open until later parts confirm,
  qualify, or overturn it. Expensive. Needed only when the text demands it.
- **Rate cost** — local predictability; how hard each new segment is to
  process.
- **Threshold cost** — ambiguity tolerance; how much unresolved openness a
  reader or system will carry before closing it early.
- **Selection pressure, not replacement** — recognition-stable content
  travels faster under the same conditions; traversal-dependent content
  survives in refugia (schools, communities, institutions) where the
  pressure is temporarily suspended.

**The four-form motif:** the novelist's own chapter, shown four times —
1. As a crashing view-count graph on the platform dashboard (Page 1)
2. As a slow-rooted tree in a rate-cost landscape, next to fast-traveling
   wildfire-like terminal content (Page 3)
3. Measured directly against terminal content on two specific axes — rate
   and threshold — on the evidence board (Page 4)
4. Found, unflagged and untargeted, simply losing a generic completion-rate
   metric in the platform's actual algorithm logs (Page 5)

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A platform's analytics dashboard, two authors' content side
by side. The novelist's serialized chapter: a jagged, declining view-count
graph. A second creator's daily self-contained post: a steady, climbing
graph. Both authors present, the novelist furious, printouts in hand.

**Panel A:** The novelist, pointing at her graph — *"Every chapter, fewer
readers finish it. Somebody's algorithm decided my work isn't worth
showing."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, looking between both graphs — *"Or
your algorithm decided something about what your writing asks of a reader.
Those aren't the same accusation."*
**Panel C:** Close on both graphs' shapes — one a fast, clean upward
curve, one a slow, ragged, declining one.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #10: ONE WRITER'S WORK IS LOSING, AND NOBODY
CAN FIND WHO'S BEATING HER

## PAGE 2 — TWO WAYS TO READ A SEGMENT

*(Directly reuses the source poster's opening image: a man saying "Yep, I
know this," versus a woman holding a book open, expression uncertain,
visibly carrying something forward.)*

**Left panel:** A reader, casual, confident — *"Yep, I know this."* Caption
beneath: RECOGNITION — classify it, match it, move on. No need to hold
anything open.

**Right panel:** A reader, deep in the novelist's book, expression
thoughtful, uncertain — holding the book slightly away from her, as if
still weighing it. Caption beneath: TRAVERSAL — carry it forward. It's not
settled yet, and it isn't supposed to be.

**Center caption:**
> Most reading is recognition. It's fast, it's cheap, and there's nothing
> wrong with it. Traversal is expensive. It's only worth it when the text
> actually demands it — and this novelist's text does.

**Bottom banner:** TWO WAYS TO READ. ONLY ONE OF THEM IS BEING MEASURED
FAIRLY HERE.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE RATE-COST LANDSCAPE

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** A landscape rendered as terrain: fast,
shallow-rooted wildfire-content spreading rapidly across flat, open ground
— catching, consuming, moving on within hours. In contrast, a small
grove of slow, deep-rooted trees, growing much more gradually, requiring
sustained attention and periodic return visits to actually take hold.
*Second appearance of the novelist's chapter — one of the deep-rooted
trees, alive and growing, just not fast, and not visible from as far away.*

**Sidebar:**
- RATE COST — how hard each new step is, moment to moment. Wildfire
  content: nearly free per step. The novelist's chapters: real, sustained
  effort per step.
- THRESHOLD COST — how much unresolved openness a reader will tolerate
  before giving up. Wildfire content: none required. The novelist's
  chapters: real ambiguity, held open on purpose, for chapters at a time.

**Bottom caption:**
> Compression works on what can close early. It fails on what has to stay
> open. Nobody built this landscape to favor one kind of growth. The
> physics of spreading just happens to.

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** two evidence cards plus the reused tortoise-and-hare panel
from the source poster, brought in directly as this issue's central image:

**RATE COST COMPARISON** — a bar chart, terminal content's per-segment
processing cost near zero, the novelist's chapters measurably higher.
Card caption: *Not a flaw in her writing. A real, honest cost her writing
asks a reader to pay.*

**THRESHOLD COST COMPARISON** — a second bar chart, terminal content's
tolerated ambiguity near zero, the novelist's chapters requiring readers to
sit with open questions across several releases.
Card caption: *Two failure modes look identical from outside: "can't carry
it" and "won't carry it." The algorithm can't tell them apart either.*

**THE RACE, RESUMED** — *(directly reused image)* the rabbit and the
tortoise on a track, the rabbit's lane relabeled RECOGNITION-STABLE
CONTENT, the tortoise's lane relabeled TRAVERSAL-DEPENDENT CONTENT.
Card caption: *Selection pressure, not replacement. The tortoise didn't
stop existing. It just stopped winning this particular race.*

**Bottom banner:** SAME TRACK. SAME RULES FOR EVERYONE. NOT THE SAME KIND
OF RUNNER.

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective and a platform engineer, reading
the actual recommendation algorithm's logs together — no rule mentioning
the novelist by name, no flag on her account, no manual demotion anywhere
in the code. Just a single generic metric: completion rate within the
first sixty seconds. *Fourth appearance of the chapter — found in the
logs, un-singled-out, simply losing a metric that was never built with
traversal content in mind.*

**Small insets:**
Novelist's fear: "Someone is deciding against me specifically."
Logs show: ✔ No targeting. ❌ A universal metric that structurally can't
see what her chapters are actually doing.

**Final panel:** The engineer, staring at the metric definition, quiet.
> Caption: Nobody wrote a rule against her. They wrote a rule that only
> knows how to reward recognition, and never asked what it would do to
> anything that wasn't.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The platform executive, defensive — *"The algorithm treats
every piece of content identically. That's the whole design principle."*
**Panel B:** The novelist — *"Identical rules aren't the same as a fair
outcome, if the rules only measure one kind of reading."*
**Panel C:** The Detective, laying Page 4's evidence between them.

**Visual centerpiece:** the rate-cost landscape from Page 3 again, this
time with a small, deliberately protected clearing drawn into it — neither
wildfire nor untouched wilderness, but a tended grove.

**Detective:**
> "You didn't build a censor. You built a metric that's honest about one
> kind of reading and blind to another. That's not malice. It's still
> going to keep happening to every writer whose work needs a reader to
> hold something open, unless you build somewhere for that kind of reading
> to survive on purpose."

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective, the rate-cost landscape behind
him, the tended grove now visible and clearly labeled.

**Icons, row:**
- RECOGNITION — settled at first contact.
- TRAVERSAL — carried forward, held open on purpose.
- RATE COST / THRESHOLD COST — the two prices traversal actually charges.

**Giant text, the source poster's own line, kept intact:** NOT ALL READING
IS DECLINING. ONLY TRAVERSAL IS.

**Small line, dual callback:**
> This is Issue 5's field again, from a different direction: nobody
> targeted her, a thousand small optimizations just happened to lean the
> same way. And it's Issue 6's structural memory, too — a text that asks a
> reader to hold something open is asking for exactly the kind of
> retained, uncollapsed state a fast metric was never built to notice.

**Bottom strip:** five domain icons, LANGUAGE now shown split into two
lanes — wildfire and grove — echoing the tortoise-and-hare image directly.

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** The platform's new dashboard, a second, separate metric
added — dwell-and-return rate across releases — measuring traversal
content on its own terms instead of forcing it through the completion-rate
lens.

**Middle panel:** A curated space on the platform, explicitly labeled and
protected, function much like the source poster's "refugia" — schools,
reading groups, and slow-release fiction given room the general feed
doesn't have to make room for.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, its beam this time shown holding
steady on one patch of water for several long seconds before moving on —
a traversal beam, not a recognition flash.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing here was suppressed on purpose. Something just cost more than
> the only ruler in the room knew how to measure.

**Footer:** NOT ALL READING IS DECLINING. ONLY TRAVERSAL IS.
