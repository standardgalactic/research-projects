# ISSUE 13 — "NOBODY DECIDED THIS, EXCEPT SOMEBODY DID"
### Institutions and Political Economy
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–12

**Case type:** The deliberate inversion of the series' recurring "no
villain" issues (2.5, 4.5, 10, 11.5). Every one of those cases taught the
reader that something suspicious-looking can be genuinely structural — no
author, no conspiracy, just honest local processes adding up. This issue
argues the reverse can also be true, and that institutions know it: **"it
just emerged" is sometimes a fact and sometimes a cover story, and telling
them apart is itself the case.**

**The loss — or rather, the excuse:** Thousands of gig delivery workers,
across dozens of unconnected cities, see their income drop by the same
percentage within the same forty-eight hours. The platform's spokesperson
gives the same answer the Detective has heard be true four times before:
*"Nobody decided this. It emerged from millions of independent local
optimizations. Nobody's sitting in a room deciding your case."* The
Detective, for the first time in the series, has good reason to doubt that
answer on arrival.

**Direct structural inversion of Issue 5:** Issue 5 opened with an accusing
councilmember convinced a hidden target existed, and the case revealed a
genuine field, no target, no author. This issue opens with an institution
offering the field explanation *first*, unprompted, and the case reveals a
genuine target hiding underneath it — one meeting, one parameter, one
signature.

**The four-form motif:** the pay-per-job parameter itself, shown four
times —
1. Experienced only as "the algorithm did it," diffuse and unowned, by
   workers across dozens of cities (Page 1)
2. Rendered as the diffuse field diagram the company offers as explanation
   — no center, no magnet — directly reusing Issue 5's visual grammar
   (Page 3)
3. Traced to a specific internal memo, dated, signed, naming the executive
   who approved it (Page 4)
4. Followed live, in the actual configuration system, from that single
   signed change down to every affected worker's payout (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A gig platform's regional office, a crowd of drivers outside
holding printed pay statements, all showing the same sharp drop on the
same date. A company spokesperson at a podium, calm, rehearsed.

**Panel A:** The spokesperson — *"The algorithm adjusts itself constantly,
based on millions of local signals. Nobody here is deciding your specific
case."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, who has heard this exact sentence be
completely true four times already this year — *"I've believed that
sentence before. I'm going to need more than the sentence this time."*
**Panel C:** Close on a dozen pay statements from a dozen different
cities, the drop identical to the decimal point, on the identical date.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #13: THE SAME EXPLANATION THAT'S BEEN TRUE
FOUR TIMES BEFORE

---

## PAGE 2 — TWO WITNESSES

**Panel A:** The spokesperson, slide deck open — *"Complex systems produce
emergent outcomes. This is well understood. Nobody controls an algorithm
this large."*
**Panel B:** A worker organizer, spreadsheet open instead — *"Emergent
outcomes don't happen on the same Tuesday, in cities that don't talk to
each other, by the exact same percentage. That's not weather. That's a
switch."*
**Center caption:**
> Complexity downstream of a decision looks identical to complexity with
> no decision at all — right up until you check the timing.

**Bottom banner:** "IT'S COMPLICATED" AND "NOBODY DECIDED THIS" ARE NOT
THE SAME CLAIM, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS BEING OFFERED HONESTLY.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE FIELD, RECHECKED

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** Two panels side by side, deliberately
reusing Issue 5's exact iron-filings visual grammar. Left panel, labeled
EXPLANATION OFFERED: the diffuse field, arrows leaning gently, no magnet
drawn — indistinguishable from Issue 5's honest version. Right panel,
labeled WHAT'S ACTUALLY THERE: the same arrows, but now traced backward
until they converge, cleanly, on a single glowing node — a parameter, not
a person, but a single point of origin none the less. *Second appearance
of the parameter — first as an innocent-looking field, then as a real
convergence point.*

**Sidebar:**
- A GENUINE FIELD — arrows that stay diffuse no matter how far back you
  trace them. (Issue 5. True there.)
- A LAUNDERED DECISION — arrows that look diffuse at first glance and
  converge the moment you trace them one step further back. (True here.)

**Bottom caption:**
> The picture on the left is real. It's just not a picture of this case.
> It's a picture of the last time this sentence was true.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** three evidence cards, each closing the distance between
"emergent" and "ordered":

**THE MEMO** — an internal document, dated, a single line: "Reduce
per-job base rate by 12%, effective the 14th," signed by name.
*Third appearance of the parameter, now traced to an actual signature.*
Card caption: *Not a policy. Not a philosophy. One line, one date, one
name.*

**THE ROLLOUT LOG** — a systems log showing the 12% change pushed to every
regional server simultaneously, at the same timestamp, across every city
in the complaint.
Card caption: *A genuine field doesn't roll out on a deploy schedule.*

**THE DOWNSTREAM COMPLEXITY** — a real, honestly complicated map of how
that single number then interacts with thousands of independent local
factors — traffic, demand, driver availability — to produce a slightly
different-looking effect in every city.
Card caption: *Everything after the memo really is complicated. That
complexity was never in dispute. It just isn't where the decision was
made.*

**Bottom banner:** THE FIELD WAS REAL. IT JUST STARTED ONE INCH TO THE
LEFT OF WHERE THEY POINTED.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective and a company engineer, single-
stepping the configuration system live — echo of Issue 8's debugger scene
— from the signed memo's line item down through the deploy pipeline to one
individual driver's actual payout, watching the number change in real
time. *Fourth appearance of the parameter — followed end to end, one
decision to one paycheck, with no honest place along the way to call it
emergent.*

**Small insets:**
Spokesperson's claim: "No one decided your specific case."
Trace shows: ✔ One decision. ✔ Applied identically to every case.

**Final panel:** The engineer, watching the number update on a live
driver's account in front of him.
> Caption: There was a moment, right here, where a signature became a pay
> cut. Nothing emergent happens at a moment like that.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** The spokesperson, still holding the line — *"The system is
genuinely complex. I'm not lying about that."*
**Panel B:** The Detective — *"You're not lying about the complexity.
You're using real complexity, that I've seen be the whole honest story
four separate times, to stand in front of one signed decision that isn't
complex at all."*
**Panel C:** The worker organizer, holding the memo up next to the pay
statements.

**Visual centerpiece:** Issue 1's off-balance scale, reused one final time
— REACHABLE weighted down as before, but this time ADMISSIBLE isn't empty
by accident. It's empty because someone with a signature made it that way,
on purpose, and described the emptiness as weather.

**Detective:**
> "Issue one of this file taught me that closing a future is a choice, and
> forgetting you made it is the problem. You didn't forget. You built a
> whole vocabulary out of the honest version of this story, specifically
> so nobody would ask whether this time was the dishonest one."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective facing the reader directly, the
two-panel field image from Page 3 behind him — genuine field on one side,
converging node on the other, both drawn in the same honest style.

**Icons, row:**
- GENUINE EMERGENCE — many small honest causes, no convergence, however
  far back you trace it.
- LAUNDERED DECISION — one real cause, wearing emergence as a costume, one
  trace-step away from being found.
- ACCOUNTABILITY — the act of tracing far enough back to tell the two
  apart, every time, even when you expect to find the first kind again.

**Giant text:** "IT EMERGED" IS SOMETIMES TRUE. INSTITUTIONS KNOW THAT, AND
SOME OF THEM SAY IT WHETHER IT'S TRUE OR NOT.

**Small line, direct callback to Issue 1:**
> Closing a future is a choice. Forgetting that you made it is the
> problem. This time, nobody forgot. Somebody was counting on everyone
> else forgetting to ask.

**Bottom strip:** five domain icons, each shown twice — once as a genuine
field, once as a laundered decision — the visual reminder that every
domain the series has toured is equally capable of both, and the only way
to tell them apart is to trace it, every time, on its own terms.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** A regulatory disclosure requirement, newly imposed: any
platform-wide parameter change above a stated threshold must be logged,
dated, and attributable to a named decision-maker, publicly.

**Middle panel:** The workers, now able to see the actual memo behind any
future pay change, rather than a field diagram standing in for one.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, its beam this time shown with a
small, clearly labeled control room at its base — visible, credited,
un-mysterious — the light still doing something that looks automatic from
a distance, but never pretending, up close, to have no one behind it.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing here was too complicated to explain. It was only ever too
> convenient not to.

**Footer:** THE FIELD WAS REAL. SOMEBODY JUST BUILT IT ON PURPOSE AND
CALLED IT WEATHER.

## Production notes

- This issue only works if the reader has genuinely trusted the "no
  villain, it's structural" explanation in 2.5, 4.5, 10, and 11.5 — the
  Detective's line on Page 1 ("I've believed that sentence before") should
  read as earned rather than cynical, precisely because it has been true,
  repeatedly, earlier in the same book. Don't let the Detective sound
  jaded going in; he's genuinely giving the company the benefit of a real
  prior track record, which makes the reveal land harder.
- Page 3's side-by-side reuse of Issue 5's exact visual grammar is the
  issue's whole argument in one image — resist the temptation to redesign
  it to look more sinister. The left panel needs to be genuinely
  indistinguishable from Issue 5's honest version, because that's the
  point: you cannot tell a real field from a laundered one by looking at
  it. You can only tell by tracing it.
- The spokesperson should not be written as a liar in the cartoonish
  sense — "I'm not lying about the complexity" is true. The actual offense
  is narrower and more damning: using a true fact about downstream
  complexity to avoid disclosing a simple, upstream, entirely ordinary
  business decision. Keep that distinction sharp in the dialogue.
