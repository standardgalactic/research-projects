# ISSUE 9 — "THE WORD BOTH SIDES STILL USE"
### Semantic Infrastructure
### Page-by-page poster format, matching Issues 1–8
### Application issue — no longer load-bearing on later issues, but draws
### directly on Issue 2 (Ecology of Distinctions) and Issue 8 (Spherepop)

**Case type:** The first of the application issues (9–13), which per the
series' dependency graph can run in any order relative to each other, since
they only depend on the completed spine (0–8). This one is the most direct
callback of the run: it takes Issue 2's taxonomy (Merge / Drift / Neglect)
and Issue 8's meld concept and applies both, explicitly, at civilizational
scale — shared vocabulary as infrastructure, aging the way a bridge or a
banknote's watermark ages.

**The loss — or rather, the dispute:** Two neighboring communities share a
two-hundred-year-old treaty granting "free passage" through a shared
corridor of land. Both sides insist they're honoring the treaty exactly as
written. Both sides are now enforcing wildly different rules at the border
— one permits foot traffic only, the other insists it always meant any
peaceful movement, vehicles included. Neither side believes the other is
reading the same four-letter phrase honestly.

**Direct callback — this issue's taxonomy is Issue 2's, reused on purpose:**
- **MERGE** — a meaning genuinely lost; no trace of the original sense
  survives anywhere.
- **DRIFT** — the word's still in use, but what it tracks has moved, and
  nobody updated the treaty alongside it.
- **NEGLECT** — the original sense is still recoverable, in living memory
  or old record, but nobody's bothered to check it in generations.

This issue argues the treaty dispute is a textbook **DRIFT** case — same
diagnosis Issue 2 made about two banknotes, now made about a phrase two
nations have been reading differently for longer than anyone currently
living has been alive.

**The four-form motif:** the phrase "free passage" itself, shown four
times —
1. Inscribed, singular and unambiguous-looking, in the original treaty
   document (Page 1)
2. Forking into two visibly different branches on a semantic drift-tree —
   direct visual sibling to Issue 2's distinction-tree (Page 3)
3. Tracked across two centuries of independent dictionary and legal-code
   entries, one per community, drifting apart entry by entry (Page 4)
4. Spoken live, in an actual sentence, by a speaker from each community —
   sincere, fluent, and irreconcilable (Page 5)

---

## PAGE 1 — THE MYSTERY

**Main art:** A border checkpoint, two delegations facing off across a
folding table. Between them, an ornate two-hundred-year-old treaty
document, open to a single underlined clause: "...free passage shall be
guaranteed in perpetuity..." Both sides have their own certified copy, word
for word identical.

**Panel A:** Delegate A, tapping the page — *"Free passage. Always meant
anyone, on foot or otherwise. We've never restricted it and neither should
you."*
**Panel B:** Close on the Detective, glancing between both identical
copies — *"You're both reading the same four words. That's not the same as
both of you meaning the same thing by them."*
**Panel C:** Close on the underlined clause itself, ink faded but legible,
identical in both certified copies.

**Bottom banner:** CASE FILE #9: TWO NATIONS, ONE TREATY, ONE PHRASE, AND
NO AGREEMENT ON WHAT IT SAYS

---

## PAGE 2 — TWO WITNESSES

**Panel A:** A linguist, archive volumes stacked around her — *"I can trace
this phrase through two centuries of court filings and dictionaries. It
didn't hold still in either language. It never does."*
**Panel B:** A village elder from one of the communities, no books, arms
folded — *"I don't need a filing to tell me what 'free passage' means. I've
heard it used correctly my whole life. That's not less real than her
archive."*
**Center caption:**
> One of them keeps the written trail. One of them keeps the living use.
> Neither of those is the counterfeit. That's Issue 2's lesson, arriving
> here at the scale of a whole language instead of two banknotes.

**Bottom banner:** WRITTEN RECORD AND LIVING SPEECH ARE TWO DIFFERENT WAYS
OF REMEMBERING A WORD. THIS TIME, THEY DISAGREE.

---

## PAGE 3 — THE SEMANTIC DRIFT-TREE

**Full page, visual centerpiece.** Issue 2's distinction-tree, redrawn at
civilizational scale: a single root labeled FREE PASSAGE, two hundred years
ago, forking gradually as it grows upward through time into two clearly
separated branches — one drifting toward FOOT TRAFFIC ONLY, one drifting
toward ANY PEACEFUL MOVEMENT. *Second appearance of the phrase — now
visibly two different words wearing one spelling.*

**Sidebar, same three categories as Issue 2, explicitly relabeled for this
case:**
- MERGED PHRASES — words that lost their original sense everywhere, with
  nothing left to recover.
- DRIFTING PHRASES — this one. Still spoken fluently on both branches.
  Tracking two different things now.
- NEGLECTED PHRASES — recoverable in principle, unchecked in practice —
  not what's happening here, but a real risk if nobody writes down what's
  just been found.

**Bottom caption:**
> A shared vocabulary is infrastructure, the same as a bridge or a road.
> It doesn't stay shared by accident. It stays shared by upkeep.

---

## PAGE 4 — THE INVESTIGATION BOARD

**Full page,** three evidence cards, each community's side of the drift
tracked independently:

**COMMUNITY A'S DICTIONARY TRAIL** — five dictionary entries across two
centuries, the definition of "passage" quietly widening decade by decade
to include first carts, then wagons, then any peaceful transit.
Card caption: *Nobody voted to widen it. It widened the way a path widens
under enough honest feet.*

**COMMUNITY B'S LEGAL-CODE TRAIL** — five legal-code entries across the
same two centuries, "passage" narrowing instead, formalized specifically to
mean foot traffic once wheeled transit became its own separately regulated
category.
Card caption: *Nobody voted to narrow it either. A new category got
invented next door to it, and this word just kept its original, smaller
job.*
*Third appearance of the phrase, now split across two independently
documented, equally honest trails.*

**THE COGNATE CHECK** — a linguist's comparison table, showing the same
root word drifting in unrelated ways in two other, unconnected languages
entirely — proof this kind of drift isn't a local grudge, it's just what
words do.
Card caption: *This has happened before, elsewhere, to nobody's benefit or
blame.*

**Bottom banner:** TWO HONEST DRIFTS. ONE DOCUMENT NEVER UPDATED TO SAY SO.

---

## PAGE 5 — WALKING THE TERRITORY

**Large vertical panel:** The Detective at the actual border crossing,
recorder in hand, asking a resident from each side to use the phrase in an
ordinary sentence, unprompted. *Fourth appearance of the phrase — spoken
live, fluently, sincerely, meaning two different things within the same
five minutes.*

**Small insets:**
Resident A: *"Free passage means my truck can cross same as my feet can."*
Resident B: *"Free passage means I can walk it. Nobody said trucks."*

**Final panel:** The Detective, recorder off, neither speaker aware they'd
just disagreed.
> Caption: Nobody here was lying. They were both fluent in a word that quit
> being one word a long time before either of them was born.

---

## PAGE 6 — THE CONFRONTATION

**Panel A:** Delegate A, accusing — *"You've been quietly reinterpreting
this for generations to shut us out."*
**Panel B:** Delegate B, equally accusing — *"You've been quietly
reinterpreting it to claim more than you were ever owed."*
**Panel C:** The Detective, laying Page 4's three cards on the table
between them.

**Visual centerpiece:** the two dictionary/legal-code trails from Page 4,
overlaid — both starting at the identical root word, both drifting steadily
and gradually, neither trail showing a single sudden jump that would
suggest deliberate manipulation.

**Detective:**
> "Neither of you moved this word on purpose. It drifted the ordinary way
> words drift — a little every generation, in a direction that made local
> sense at the time. Nobody maintained the treaty alongside the language it
> was written in. That's not treachery. That's Issue 2's neglect, except
> the thing left unmaintained wasn't a banknote. It was a shared word."

---

## PAGE 7 — THE FRAMEWORK (manifesto page)

**Large central figure:** The Detective facing the reader, the semantic
drift-tree from Page 3 behind him, root and both branches fully labeled.

**Same three icons as Issue 2, explicitly reused rather than redesigned:**
- MERGE — two shapes fused into one.
- DRIFT — a fork with the label pointing the wrong direction.
- NEGLECT — a locked, dusty cabinet.

**Giant text:** SHARED VOCABULARY IS INFRASTRUCTURE. IT DOES NOT MAINTAIN
ITSELF.

**Small line, explicit dual callback:**
> This is Issue 2's taxonomy again, because it was never really about
> banknotes. And it's Issue 8's problem again, too: two histories, melded
> into one treaty, with nobody checking what got discarded when the words
> drifted apart underneath it.

**Bottom strip:** the five domain icons, city/codebase/brain/institution/
language, with LANGUAGE now drawn larger and central rather than as one
icon among five — the domain this issue is actually about, foregrounded
for the first time.

---

## PAGE 8 — RESOLUTION

**Top panel:** A new appendix, signed by both delegations, added to the
treaty: not a single new definition replacing the old one, but a **meld**
in Issue 8's sense — both current meanings recorded side by side, neither
overwriting the other, both cross-referenced to the shared root.

**Middle panel:** The border crossing, new signage in both languages,
specifying explicitly what each side's certified meaning permits, rather
than relying on one word to silently carry two different jobs.

**Large final panel:** The Lighthouse, its beam this time shown splitting
briefly through mist into two distinguishable colors before recombining
further out — two readings of the same light, both real, both reaching the
water.

**Final caption:**
> Nothing here needed a villain. A word just got old the way words do, on
> both sides, honestly, and nobody had written down that it had.

**Footer:** THEY WERE NEVER READING DIFFERENT TREATIES. THEY WERE READING
THE SAME WORD, AGED TWO DIFFERENT WAYS.

## Production notes

- This issue works best if a returning reader clocks the Page 3 tree as
  Issue 2's tree before the sidebar explicitly says so — the visual rhyme
  should arrive a beat before the text confirms it, same discipline as
  every prior deliberate callback in the series.
- Keep both delegations equally sympathetic and equally certain they're
  right — same fairness rule as every issue since 2.5. If either side reads
  as cynical or self-serving, the "honest independent drift" thesis
  collapses back into an ordinary treaty-dispute story.
- Page 8's appendix is a soft, textual echo of Issue 8's meld — worth
  letting the connection stay implicit in the art (two columns, side by
  side, neither struck through) rather than re-explaining meld itself,
  since Page 7 has already made the callback explicit in words.
