# State of the Research

## Collagen, prenatal movement, developmental organization, and longevity

**Research outline · 24 September 2026**

## 1. Scope

This report outlines the present state of a research program connecting collagen biology with Motile Womb Theory, innate flying dreams, developmental organization, sexual dimorphism, and longevity. Its purpose is to distinguish what the existing theory already proposes, what contemporary biology directly supports, what can be joined by a plausible mechanistic bridge, and what remains an open hypothesis.

The central proposal of Motile Womb Theory is that prenatal movement is not merely an output of development. Movement supplies structured sensorimotor experience through which the developing organism begins to calibrate orientation, bodily extent, three-dimensional displacement, resistance, acceleration, and the distinction between self-produced and externally produced motion. The associated idea of innate flying dreams proposes that characteristic dream experiences of suspension, falling, gliding, or flight may preserve or reconstruct an early mode of spatial experience formed before terrestrial locomotion.

The collagen branch adds a material question: whether extracellular matrices help convert developmental movement into lasting anatomical and sensory organization. Collagen is not treated as a single substance. The relevant objects are collagen gene products, tissue-specific isoforms, procollagen processing, fibril and basement-membrane assembly, receptor-binding sequences, endogenous cleavage fragments, and the mechanical properties of the matrices they form.

## 2. Present conceptual architecture

### 2.1 Motile Womb Theory

The theory currently contains four connected claims:

1. The prenatal organism develops within a moving environment generated by fetal motion, maternal motion, fluid displacement, uterine constraint, gravity, and contact with surrounding tissues.
2. These motions provide correlated vestibular, proprioceptive, tactile, interoceptive, and motor information before postnatal vision and locomotion become dominant.
3. Repeated correlations between action and sensation may contribute to primitive spatial calibration and body-world differentiation.
4. Later spatial imagination and characteristic dream motifs may reuse this early sensorimotor grammar.

The first two claims are compatible with established developmental physiology. The third is a research hypothesis with adjacent support from sensorimotor and vestibular development. The fourth is presently phenomenological and lacks a direct evidentiary chain from prenatal experience to adult dream content.

### 2.2 The collagen hypothesis

The collagen hypothesis is best stated as a mediator hypothesis rather than a sequence-determinist theory:

> Developmental movement applies forces to tissues; extracellular matrices distribute, resist, and record those forces; cells sense the resulting matrix state; and remodeling changes the future possibilities of growth and movement.

Collagen could therefore participate in a feedback loop:

\[
\text{movement}\rightarrow\text{tissue strain}\rightarrow
\text{matrix deformation}\rightarrow\text{cellular mechanosensing}
\rightarrow\text{matrix remodeling}\rightarrow
\text{altered movement and growth}.
\]

This formulation is stronger than claiming that collagen peptide sequences independently encode body plans. Sequence matters, but its developmental effect is conditional on expression, chain assembly, modification, cleavage, tissue location, receptor availability, loading, and developmental time.

### 2.3 Sexual dimorphism

The current program proposes that sexually differentiated regulatory systems may alter extracellular-matrix production or remodeling, thereby contributing to differences in body form. A direct precedent exists in *Drosophila*: sex-specific isoforms of the transcription factor Doublesex regulate collagen IV production and secretion, partly through procollagen lysyl hydroxylase, allowing a sex-determination pathway to alter basement membranes beyond the cells in which the initiating factor is expressed.

This supports the limited proposition that sex-determination pathways can control collagen biology and remotely influence tissue environments. It does not yet establish that collagen peptide sequence variation is a general driver of human sexual dimorphism. In mammals, the stronger immediate questions concern sex-biased expression, post-translational processing, crosslinking, turnover, hormonal regulation, and tissue-specific matrix composition.

## 3. Established developmental biology

### 3.1 Fetal movement is morphogenetically consequential

Reduced or absent fetal movement is associated with abnormal skeletal development, including joint contractures, altered joint shape, delayed ossification, and other defects. Animal experiments identify mechanosensitive pathways, including PIEZO and TRPV4 signaling, through which movement-dependent forces influence developing cartilage, bone, and joints. Recent mouse work further indicates that mechanical stimulation associated with maternal exercise can modify fetal skeletal development and partially rescue defects caused by fetal akinesia.

This establishes a strong version of one Motile Womb premise: movement participates causally in construction of the developing body. It does not merely exercise an already completed anatomy.

### 3.2 The uterus is a constrained mechanical environment

Biomechanical models of fetal movement show that uterine geometry, fetal position, available space, tissue contact, and amniotic-fluid conditions affect the forces produced during movement. The prenatal environment is therefore neither free flight nor ordinary weight-bearing locomotion. It is a deformable, fluid-mediated, increasingly constrained sensorimotor field.

This supplies a precise interpretation of “motile womb”: a changing boundary-value problem in which fetal and maternal movements jointly determine the mechanical input reaching the developing organism.

### 3.3 Vestibular calibration has developmental timing

The mammalian vestibular system develops through prenatal and postnatal stages, and research involving altered gravity supports sensitive-period effects on vestibular structures and functions. Vestibular development contributes to posture, homeostasis, motor control, and spatial memory. This makes prenatal motion a plausible source of early calibration, although existing evidence does not show that fetuses form an adult-like three-dimensional cognitive map or that such calibration causes later flying dreams.

### 3.4 Extracellular matrix is active developmental infrastructure

The extracellular matrix provides scaffolding, force transmission, growth-factor sequestration, receptor engagement, and spatial constraints on cell migration and tissue deformation. During embryogenesis, collagen production and processing are tissue-specific. Mouse studies of ADAMTS proteases demonstrate that related procollagen-processing enzymes acquire distinct developmental roles through their spatial correspondence with particular collagen substrates.

Individual collagens also participate in specific developmental signaling contexts. Collagen XV, for example, affects zebrafish notochord differentiation and muscle development and interacts with Hedgehog-dependent patterning. Embryo attachment and outgrowth experiments show that the biological consequences of collagen can depend on exposed binding sequences such as RGD, collagen type, and conformational state.

The supported conclusion is that collagen matrices are not passive packing. They form part of the causal environment through which morphogens, forces, adhesion, and cell behavior are coordinated.

## 4. The peptide question

“Collagen peptide” must be resolved into distinct biological categories before the theory can be tested:

1. **Encoded collagen chains and domains.** These are gene products whose sequences affect folding, trimer selection, receptor binding, fibril formation, and tissue specialization.
2. **Procollagen propeptides and processing products.** These arise during biosynthesis and may serve as biomarkers or participate in regulation.
3. **Endogenous collagen fragments or matrikines.** Proteolysis can expose or release bioactive sequences that signal during remodeling, inflammation, angiogenesis, repair, and disease.
4. **Synthetic collagen-binding or collagen-hybridizing peptides.** These are experimental and therapeutic tools used to recognize damaged or unfolded collagen.

The developmental theory is presently strongest for the first two categories and plausible but underdeveloped for endogenous fragments. A serious sequence-level program would need to identify exact peptides, their producing proteases, concentrations, receptors, tissue distributions, and developmental time windows.

## 5. Innate flying dreams

### 5.1 Current status

The existing theory interprets flying dreams as possible reconstructions of prenatal spatial experience: buoyancy without walking, rotation without a stable horizon, whole-body translation generated partly by another organism, and motion within compliant boundaries. This is an original phenomenological hypothesis rather than an established result in dream science.

### 5.2 Plausible bridge

The plausible bridge is not prenatal episodic memory. It is persistence of sensorimotor priors or generative constraints. Early vestibular and proprioceptive calibration could shape later expectations about acceleration, orientation, bodily suspension, and controllability without preserving a narratively accessible memory of the womb.

### 5.3 Missing evidence

The theory currently lacks evidence that variation in prenatal movement predicts flying-dream frequency or structure. It also lacks a discriminating account separating prenatal calibration from postnatal experiences such as being carried, rocked, swimming, falling, climbing, using swings, viewing flight, or playing spatially immersive games.

The claim should therefore remain: prenatal sensorimotor development offers a possible source of later dream geometry, not a demonstrated origin of flying dreams.

## 6. Implications for developmental biology

### 6.1 Development as reciprocal construction

The combined framework treats anatomy and activity as reciprocally produced. Genes and signaling pathways establish tissues capable of movement; movement then changes forces, matrix organization, mechanosensitive signaling, and subsequent growth. Development is consequently not a one-way passage from genome to form.

### 6.2 Matrix as developmental memory

Extracellular matrix can preserve consequences of prior loading through alignment, crosslinking, turnover, cleavage, and altered ligand presentation. “Memory” should initially be used in this material sense: earlier events change the matrix state that conditions later cell behavior. Whether this deserves a stronger informational or cognitive interpretation is a separate question.

### 6.3 Distributed sexual differentiation

The *Drosophila* collagen-IV result suggests a mechanism by which sex-specific regulation in one tissue can alter basement membranes elsewhere. This supports investigation of sexual differentiation as a distributed extracellular process, rather than only a cell-autonomous transcriptional program or a late hormonal overlay.

### 6.4 Developmental constraint and body-plan plasticity

Different matrices can change which deformations, migrations, and growth trajectories are mechanically available. Collagen composition and organization may therefore help define a space of admissible body-plan transformations. This is compatible with developmental constraint theory, but sequence-level causal claims require comparative and perturbational evidence.

## 7. Implications for longevity research

### 7.1 The extracellular matrix is an aging system

Aging changes collagen abundance, crosslinking, proteolysis, glycation, organization, and coupling to cells. These changes affect more than appearance: they alter mechanical feedback, barrier function, tissue repair, stem-cell niches, vascular behavior, and communication between cells and their surroundings.

### 7.2 Longevity interventions and matrix maintenance

In *C. elegans*, multiple longevity interventions preserve subsets of cuticular collagens and collagen-remodeling enzymes. Experimental collagen overexpression can extend lifespan in some contexts, while disruption of collagen stabilization or matrix coupling can blunt longevity produced by reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling. This is causal evidence in a nematode, not proof that increasing human collagen extends life.

The broader implication is that longevity may require continued maintenance of the organism’s mechanical and extracellular coupling, not merely protection of intracellular molecules.

### 7.3 Development and aging as one matrix trajectory

The research program’s most promising synthesis is that embryogenesis and aging occupy opposite portions of a continuous matrix-maintenance problem. Development constructs and calibrates tissue coupling; adulthood maintains it; aging progressively loses the ability to remodel it coherently. Longevity interventions may succeed partly when they preserve adaptive matrix feedback rather than merely maximizing collagen quantity.

### 7.4 Peptides as readouts and interventions

Collagen fragments could serve as indicators of tissue-specific turnover, while collagen-targeting peptides may deliver imaging agents or therapeutics to damaged matrices. Their value will depend on distinguishing causal signals from degradation products and on avoiding the assumption that any fragment derived from collagen is beneficial.

## 8. Evidence map

### Established or strongly supported

- Fetal movement generates forces required for normal skeletal morphogenesis.
- Prenatal and early postnatal vestibular development is sensitive to environmental input and altered gravity.
- Collagen-rich extracellular matrices transmit force, organize adhesion, interact with signaling systems, and undergo tissue-specific processing during embryogenesis.
- Sex-determination machinery can regulate collagen production and secretion in at least one well-characterized animal model.
- Extracellular-matrix maintenance is causally involved in some longevity pathways in *C. elegans*.

### Plausible but incompletely established

- Collagen organization mediates some of the long-term anatomical consequences of prenatal movement.
- Sex-biased matrix regulation contributes broadly to sexually dimorphic body plans.
- Developmental matrix state acts as a durable material memory that constrains later morphology and repair.
- Conserved principles of matrix maintenance connect development, healthspan, and aging across species.

### Open hypotheses

- Particular collagen peptide sequences systematically organize sexual dimorphism across species.
- Prenatal movement establishes spatial priors that later appear as flying dreams.
- Differences in maternal movement produce measurable long-term differences in spatial cognition through vestibular–matrix coupling.
- Endogenous collagen fragments coordinate developmental patterning in the specific way proposed by the theory.
- Manipulating selected matrix signals can extend mammalian lifespan rather than only improve local tissue function or healthspan.

## 9. Research program

### 9.1 Define the objects

Construct a controlled vocabulary distinguishing collagen genes, chains, domains, propeptides, endogenous fragments, modifications, receptors, proteases, and matrix architectures. Each claim should identify the exact biological object rather than use “collagen peptide” generically.

### 9.2 Build a developmental atlas

For selected model organisms, map collagen and collagen-processing gene expression across developmental time, sex, tissue, and mechanical context. Integrate spatial transcriptomics, proteomics, matrix imaging, and known morphogen pathways.

### 9.3 Test movement–matrix coupling

Use fetal-akinesia, altered-loading, organoid, embryo-culture, or animal models to ask whether changes in movement alter collagen alignment, processing, crosslinking, turnover, receptor signaling, and later tissue form. Distinguish movement-generated effects from placental, endocrine, nutritional, and maternal-stress effects.

### 9.4 Test sexual-dimorphism mechanisms

Begin with explicit causal chains:

\[
\text{sex regulator or hormone}\rightarrow
\text{collagen synthesis/processing}\rightarrow
\text{matrix property}\rightarrow
\text{dimorphic tissue outcome}.
\]

Comparative genomics alone will not establish this chain. Perturbation, rescue, tissue specificity, and developmental timing are required.

### 9.5 Test the dream hypothesis separately

Develop a preregistered phenomenology of flying dreams that records trajectory, controllability, gravity, enclosure, rotation, viewpoint, bodily effort, and affect. Only then examine associations with prenatal or early-life movement proxies. The dream hypothesis should not be treated as validation of the collagen hypothesis, or vice versa.

### 9.6 Connect to longevity without collapsing species

Identify which matrix-maintenance mechanisms are conserved from nematode cuticle to mammalian basement membranes and interstitial matrices. Prioritize mechanical coupling, proteostasis, collagen processing, integrin signaling, and turnover rather than assuming that total collagen abundance is the relevant variable.

## 10. Present assessment

The research program has a coherent central insight: development occurs in a mechanically active environment, and extracellular matrix can mediate the conversion of movement into persistent biological organization. Contemporary developmental biology supports the importance of fetal movement, mechanotransduction, tissue-specific collagen processing, and active extracellular matrices. It also supplies a concrete precedent connecting sex-determination machinery to collagen regulation and causal evidence connecting matrix maintenance to longevity in *C. elegans*.

The program does not yet establish that collagen peptide sequences constitute a general grammar of sexual dimorphism, that prenatal motion is encoded as adult flying dreams, or that collagen-directed intervention will extend human lifespan. Those are distinct hypotheses requiring distinct evidence. The immediate opportunity is to make the middle of the theory explicit: movement changes matrix state; matrix state changes cellular decisions; those decisions alter subsequent morphology, sensory calibration, and capacity for repair.
